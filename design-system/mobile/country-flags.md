# Country flags

Country flags help people recognize a country or territory in a list or control.
Pair them with a country name; a flag alone should not stand for a language.

## Appearance

Use circular artwork with vivid, recognizable color families. Preserve symbols,
proportions, and distinctions between light and dark colors. The treatment follows
Mateo's [color foundation](../foundation/color-palette.md) without restricting
national artwork to its palette or the product accent.

The artwork owns these fixed colors:

| Color family | Color |
| --- | --- |
| Red | `#F20D32` |
| Deep red | `#BF1230` |
| Royal blue | `#0866DB` |
| Light blue | `#2994FF` |
| Green | `#62B52B` |
| Dark green | `#397C24` |
| Yellow | `#FFD000` |
| Orange | `#FF9200` |
| Maroon | `#861C48` |
| Navy | `#073780` |
| Soft white | `#EEEEEE` |
| Charcoal | `#333333` |
| Detail gray | `#ACABB1` |

Soft white keeps white flag sections visible on white surfaces. Keep maroon,
navy, deep red, and dark green distinct from their brighter relatives. Preserve
small emblem details rather than flattening every region into a primary color.
Flags retain these colors when a product changes its accent.

## Use

Use a flag beside “Brazil” in a country picker. Do not use it alone to mean
“Portuguese”, or recolor it to indicate selection, success, or an error.
Selection belongs to the surrounding control.

Keep artwork still and upright, including in right-to-left layouts. When nearby
text identifies the country, the flag is decorative for screen readers. When
it stands alone, provide a localized country label. These rules apply on both
iOS and Android.
